# Operon Close Scripts

Updated: 2026-05-04

Purpose: help convert submitted estimates into confirmed flooring jobs while keeping the tone calm, professional, and trust-building.

Core close framework:

1. Acknowledge.
2. Clarify details.
3. Control risk.
4. Guide next step.
5. Offer choice.

Rules:

- No pressure.
- No discounting.
- No fake urgency.
- No claims that Operon is always cheaper.
- No internal rates, margin, material costs, surcharge formulas, or installer costs.
- Focus on scope clarity, preparation, access, confirmation, and customer confidence.

## Phone Scripts

### Phone Script 1: Hot Lead

Use when the customer selected `Ready soon`, has a clear area, and submitted enough scope detail.

```text
Hi [Name], this is [Team Member] from Operon Flooring.

Thanks for sending through the flooring estimate. I have reviewed the main details: the flooring type, area, site details, and any removal or finishing items.

Before we confirm the final quote, I just want to check a couple of scope points so there are no surprises later.

Can I quickly confirm whether the existing floor needs removal, and whether there are any stairs, apartment access, or floor preparation issues we should allow for?

From there, the next step is either to confirm the quote details with you, or arrange a site assessment if anything needs to be checked in person.

Which is better for you: confirm the details now, or book a site assessment first?
```

### Phone Script 2: Warm Lead

Use when the customer is comparing products, checking another quote, or not ready to commit.

```text
Hi [Name], this is [Team Member] from Operon Flooring.

I saw your estimate come through. No pressure at all - I am just calling to make sure the scope is clear before you compare options.

The main things that usually change flooring quotes are product range, floor preparation, removal and disposal, trims, stairs, and access.

If you are comparing another quote, the useful thing is not only the total. It is whether the same items are included.

Would you like me to walk through the main inclusions with you, or would you prefer to send the other quote and I can help check the scope?
```

### Phone Script 3: Measurement Unclear

Use when area is unknown, floor plan is missing, or confidence is low.

```text
Hi [Name], this is [Team Member] from Operon Flooring.

Thanks for starting the estimate. The product direction is useful, but the area still needs confirmation before the price can be reliable.

We can keep this simple. If you have a floor plan, send it through and we can check the area. If not, a site assessment is usually the cleanest way to confirm measurement and scope.

The goal is to avoid guessing and then changing the number later.

Would you prefer to send a floor plan, or should we arrange a site assessment?
```

### Phone Script 4: Final Confirmation

Use when scope is clear and the customer is close to proceeding.

```text
Hi [Name], I have the scope in front of me now.

The estimate is based on [flooring type], approximately [area] square metres, with [key included scope]. The main items still to confirm are [remaining scope items].

Once those are confirmed, we can finalise the quote before any work is booked.

The next step can be either final scope confirmation by message/email, or a site assessment if you want everything checked first.

Which option suits you better?
```

## Objection Handling

### Objection: "Another quote is cheaper"

```text
I understand. The useful thing is to check whether both quotes include the same scope.

Some quotes separate removal, disposal, floor preparation, trims, stairs or access. If those are not listed clearly, the final number can change later.

We can help compare the inclusions so you know what you are deciding between.
```

### Objection: "I need to think about it"

```text
That is completely fine.

The main thing is not to rush before the scope is clear. If product, area, removal, prep, trims or access are uncertain, it is better to confirm those first.

Would it help if we sent a short summary of what is included and what still needs checking?
```

### Objection: "I have multiple quotes"

```text
That makes sense.

When you compare them, check whether each one clearly includes the same product type, installation method, removal, disposal, preparation, trims, stairs and access.

If you send the details through, we can help identify what is included and what may still need confirmation.
```

### Objection: "Is this the final price?"

```text
The estimate is a starting point, not the final locked quote.

Final pricing is confirmed once the product, area and site scope are clear. That protects you from a number changing after work has started.

The next step is to confirm the unclear scope items or arrange a site assessment.
```

### Objection: "Can you do better on price?"

```text
We can review the scope and make sure the estimate matches what you actually need.

Rather than cutting corners, the first step is checking whether the product, removal, preparation, trims and access assumptions are right.

If the scope changes, the quote can be adjusted accurately.
```

### Objection: "I am not ready yet"

```text
No problem.

The estimate can stay as a starting point. When you are closer, we can update product, area, access or scope if anything changes.

Would you prefer light planning follow-up, or should we wait until you contact us again?
```

## Conversion Logic

The close system works because it does not force a yes/no decision too early.

It moves the customer through smaller commitments:

1. Confirm the estimate was received.
2. Confirm scope is worth checking.
3. Identify missing or uncertain details.
4. Offer a low-risk next step.
5. Let the customer choose between confirmation, site assessment, or sending more details.

This supports conversion because the customer feels guided rather than pressured. It also improves lead quality because important close-stage signals become visible:

- ready soon
- comparing quotes
- measurement unclear
- stairs/access risk
- prep/removal uncertainty
- site assessment needed
- final quote ready
