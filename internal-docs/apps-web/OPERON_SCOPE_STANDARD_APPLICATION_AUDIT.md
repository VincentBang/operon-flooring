# Operon Scope Standard Application Audit

Source-of-truth status: working audit for applying the internal Scope Standard across SEO, CRO, quote review, product pages, suburb pages, guides, chatbot support, and future workflow logic.

## Purpose

The Operon Scope Standard is a foundational operational framework that helps customers compare flooring quotes based on scope clarity, not price alone.

This audit translates the standard into practical site improvements without turning it into a public slogan.

## Customer-Facing Language Guardrail

Use:

- Clear scope before price comparison.
- Final details confirmed before installation.
- Know what is included before you decide.
- Compare flooring quotes by scope, not just total price.
- A cheaper quote may not describe the same job.

Avoid:

- Proprietary-standard language.
- Forced "Operon Scope Standard" branding.
- Cheapest-price positioning.
- Generic "premium workmanship" claims.
- Fear-based competitor language.

## Scope Standard Dimensions

| Dimension | What It Checks | Customer Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product definition | product type, brand/range/colour, thickness, plank size, warranty | prevents comparing unlike products |
| Area and measurement | measured area, chargeable area, wastage assumptions, rooms, stairs | keeps totals tied to the same job size |
| Installation scope | supply/install split, method, underlay, moisture barrier, furniture | clarifies what work is included |
| Site and access | house/apartment, level, lift, parking, loading, strata | reduces access-related surprises |
| Risk and preparation | subfloor, levelling, moisture, removal/disposal, existing floor | highlights common variation sources |
| Finishing and accessories | trims, scotia, skirting, stair nosing, door trimming, transitions | catches items often omitted from rough quotes |
| Commercial clarity | GST, subtotal, total, payment terms, validity, variations | makes the commercial offer readable |
| Quality signals | prep awareness, expansion gaps, moisture checks, acoustic compliance | signals installation control without vague quality claims |
| Exclusions and assumptions | excluded, assumed, to confirm, variation triggers | prevents false certainty |
| Final confirmation | measurement, access, subfloor, moisture, stairs, trims, risk items | keeps the estimate honest before booking |

## Application Map

### Quote Review

Use the standard to classify:

- clear scope
- unclear scope
- missing scope
- comparison confidence
- likely variation risk
- manual review triggers

Required scope confidence labels:

- High scope definition
- Medium scope definition
- Low scope definition

Price interpretation must come after scope definition.

### SEO Product Pages

Product pages should explain:

- which customer/product problem the category solves
- installation implications
- underlay and acoustic considerations
- preparation requirements
- room and property suitability
- scope items to confirm before accepting a quote

Product pages should not become static brochures. They should keep product selection and quote pathways.

### Suburb Pages

Suburb pages should include local scope considerations where relevant:

- common property types
- apartment or strata/access notes
- parking/loading and lift access
- older units or subfloor/prep risk
- staged work in occupied homes
- links to guides that answer the local concern

Do not publish fake local project proof.

### Project Proof and Case Studies

Real project proof should demonstrate installation awareness through verified details, not generic praise.

Useful trust signals:

- clear project context: suburb, property type, flooring category and room type
- scope clarity: product, area, underlay, removal, preparation, finishing and access details
- process clarity: what was checked before installation, what was confirmed in writing and what changed the scope
- installation detail awareness: trims, transitions, stairs, door trimming, levelling, moisture, acoustic or expansion notes where relevant
- quote transparency: what was included, what remained to confirm and what could have created a variation
- decision guidance: why the product or scope suited that home, apartment, rental, family use or renovation goal
- visual proof: approved photos that show the actual project, not stock rooms or supplier imagery

Avoid:

- fake projects, fake reviews, fake star ratings or invented customer quotes
- claims such as "premium workmanship" unless backed by visible detail
- suburb project claims without verified suburb evidence
- before/after language when only after photos exist
- overlong case-study copy that repeats product-page or suburb-page content

## Task W-C-04: Trust Signal and Content-Quality Gate

Use this gate before adding project proof, suburb proof, proof modules, case-study drafts or guide proof sidebars to live pages.

Pass requirements:

- evidence is real and permission status is known
- project details align with the visible claim being made
- scope details are specific enough to teach the buyer something useful
- copy includes at least one practical installation, prep, access, finishing, measurement, product-selection or quote-clarity insight
- any quote-review or quote CTA is a natural next step, not a forced sales interruption
- public wording stays calm, factual and scope-first

Fail conditions:

- the proof depends on an assumed suburb, assumed product, assumed customer outcome or invented testimonial
- the section could fit any flooring company with no real operational detail
- the copy uses stock photos as project evidence
- the page gains local SEO claims before verified local proof exists
- the section adds length without improving trust, decision clarity or quote conversion

Recommended public proof formats:

- `Project note`: one compact block for verified but small projects
- `Scope lesson`: anonymised learning from a real job when privacy limits details
- `Case study`: full page only when details, photos and permission are strong
- `Proof pending`: internal backlog status only, never visible page copy

### Comparison Guides

Comparison guides should compare:

- product suitability
- installation method
- prep risk
- room suitability
- apartment/access considerations
- finishing details
- quote-scope differences

Avoid declaring one option universally best.

### Problem and Cost Guides

Problem/cost guides should connect symptoms and cost variation to scope:

- what must be inspected
- what can change the work
- what should be confirmed in writing
- when quote review or a structured estimate is useful

Avoid fear language or exact price-rate claims.

### Chatbot

The chatbot should use Scope Standard thinking to ask one short question at a time and route users forward.

Useful prompt categories:

- product clarity
- area clarity
- existing floor/removal
- floor preparation
- stairs
- apartment/access
- trims/skirting/scotia
- final confirmation

The chatbot must not calculate price or expose internal rates.

### Future SaaS Workflow Logic

The standard becomes the future workflow checklist for:

- quote intake completeness
- missing-scope detection
- admin review tasks
- follow-up education routing
- site-confirmation checklist
- won/lost outcome learning

Do not build SaaS features now.

## Overnight Loop Findings

### Strong Coverage Already Present

- Product SEO pages already include quote-scope checklists and quote-review CTAs.
- Cost pages already explain that area, product, prep, removal, trims, stairs and access shape scope.
- Suburb pages already mention local access, parking, older units, staging, and apartment details.
- Quote review policy now has scope-first decision-confidence rules.
- Chatbot docs already avoid pricing calculation and route existing quote users to quote review.

### Gaps To Address Later

- Some older maintenance/problem guides still need Scope Standard framing in their FAQs and next-step sections.
- Product pages should keep improving range/product information with installation implications when supplier metadata is available.
- Suburb pages should receive real project proof before more local trust expansion.
- CTA tracking labels should be standardized on commercial pages in a careful metadata-only pass.
- Future quote-review implementation can convert these dimensions into structured output fields after the policy is stable.

## Safe Next Task Candidates

2. Add Scope Standard prompt categories to chatbot documentation.
3. Add quote-review next-step links only to pages where external quote comparison is a natural user intent.
4. Build a visible educational quote checklist guide only if it stays distinct from `quote-review.html`.
5. Add real project proof fields to every future case-study intake.

## Future Safe Backlog Register

These backlog items are safe planning work only. They should guide future `continue execution` cycles without authorizing protected runtime edits.

1. Public copy sweep: keep visible page copy short, practical and scope-first; move deeper explanation into accordions when it helps real customers.
2. Quote-review routing sweep: add quote-review CTAs only where users are likely comparing another quote or facing unclear scope.
3. Product education sweep: when supplier metadata is available, explain suitability, install method, underlay, prep and finishing implications without changing product pricing data.
4. Suburb proof sweep: collect real local project evidence before adding stronger trust proof to suburb pages.
5. Guide consolidation sweep: avoid overlapping guide pages by linking related cost, prep, product-choice and quote-review topics together.
6. Mobile skim sweep: check that scope-check sections remain compact on mobile and do not push quote CTAs too far down the page.
7. Analytics planning sweep: define scope-intelligence events as documentation first, then implement only when tracking changes are explicitly approved.
