# Operon Site Copy Policy

Source-of-truth status: active copy, UX, CRO, and SEO policy.

Last updated: 2026-05-08

## Core Copy Principle

Operon copy must make the customer feel guided, not overloaded.

Visible page copy should be short, premium, practical, and easy to scan. Deeper explanation belongs in accordions, FAQs, guide pages, or dedicated decision-support content.

Do not use copy to expose internal rates, internal pricing rules, service-role logic, or backend implementation detail.

## Operon Scope Standard Copy Policy

The Operon Scope Standard is a foundational operational framework that helps customers compare flooring quotes based on scope clarity, not price alone.

Use it quietly to shape language, structure, UX, quote review, product pages, suburb pages, comparison guides, and educational content.

Do not over-market it publicly as a named framework.

Avoid:

- "Our proprietary scope standard"
- "Operon Scope Standard(TM)"
- "We are the best"
- "Premium workmanship"
- "Cheapest quote"

Prefer:

- "Clear scope before price comparison"
- "Final details confirmed before installation"
- "Know what is included before you decide"
- "Compare flooring quotes by scope, not just total price"
- "A cheaper quote may not describe the same job"

Customer-facing content should not behave like generic flooring marketing. It should behave like structured flooring decision guidance.

Every major content page should help customers understand at least one of:

- product choice
- installation scope
- quote comparison
- hidden cost risk
- preparation risk
- apartment or site constraints
- finishing detail
- final confirmation process

Apply the Scope Standard by page type:

- quote review: classify clear, missing, unclear, and risky scope before price comparison
- quote UX: ask enough to support structured scope without making the form feel complex
- product pages: explain product fit, installation implications, underlay/prep considerations, suitability, and quote-scope items
- suburb pages: explain local scope considerations such as apartments, strata/acoustic expectations, access, parking, delivery, common housing types, and real project context where available
- comparison guides: compare practical scope, installation implications, and decision tradeoffs before price
- marketing copy: reinforce clarity, professionalism, structured quoting, reduced surprises, and installation confidence
- video/content strategy: create useful topics such as why two flooring quotes describe different jobs, what should be included in a flooring quote, why cheap-looking flooring quotes can change, what floor preparation means, and what apartment owners should check before choosing flooring

## Progressive Disclosure Copy Policy

Operon uses a layered copy system:

### Layer 1 - Visible Surface Copy

Visible homepage, hero, card, CTA, and section copy must be:

- short
- premium
- direct
- easy to scan
- not repetitive
- not overexplained

Good examples:

- "Start with a structured estimate."
- "Final details confirmed before installation."
- "Quote, products, review, or practical guides."

Avoid:

- long paragraphs under headings
- repeated helper text
- defensive explanations
- explaining the same quote process multiple times

### Layer 2 - Expandable Explanation Copy

Accordions, FAQ blocks, details/summary blocks, and hidden explanation panels may be more detailed.

Use expandable sections for:

- quote process explanations
- flooring scope clarification
- product tradeoffs
- installation risks
- underlay/acoustic layer explanations
- floor preparation issues
- quote review guidance
- apartment flooring questions
- cost variation explanations
- maintenance guidance

Expandable content should:

- answer real customer questions
- support SEO with useful semantic depth
- reduce uncertainty
- improve trust
- guide the next action
- remain readable on mobile

Expandable content may be reasonably wordy when the added detail helps the customer make a better decision.

Use this hidden-copy style:

- 2 to 4 short sentences is usually appropriate
- explain the practical reason, not just the headline answer
- add context that helps comparison, preparation, or expectation-setting
- sound experienced and educational, not salesy
- keep paragraphs readable and plain-English on mobile
- avoid one-line placeholder answers on guide pages, especially generic lines like "Simple care helps avoid bigger issues", "Review quote clarity before deciding", or "Cost depends on area, product and scope"

Good hidden-answer pattern:

- direct answer first
- practical explanation second
- decision guidance or next step third when useful

FAQ answer minimum standard:

- every guide FAQ answer should give a direct answer plus the practical reason behind it
- most guide FAQ answers should be 2 to 4 short sentences, not a single generic sentence
- include the decision implication when the question affects product choice, repair vs replacement, preparation, quote scope, apartment/access constraints, or maintenance risk
- visible FAQ answers and FAQPage schema answers must be updated together when schema is present
- short binary answers are allowed only when the page already provides the needed context immediately beside the FAQ

Examples:

- explain why a quote can change, not just that it can change
- explain what a complete quote should list, not just that it needs confirmation
- explain when a floor plan is useful, what it helps with, and what it does not confirm
- explain underlay, acoustic, prep, moisture, stairs, trims, and scope items in practical trade language

Do not use expandable sections for:

- keyword stuffing
- fake FAQ volume
- generic SEO filler
- repeated copy across pages
- hiding all important page meaning

### Layer 3 - Dedicated Deep Pages

If an expandable answer becomes too long, create or link to a dedicated guide page.

Examples:

- hybrid vs laminate
- why flooring quotes vary
- flooring installation cost guide
- underlay and acoustic layer guide
- scotia vs skirting
- apartment flooring guide
- quote review checklist

Rule:

Accordions should support the page.
Dedicated guides should carry deep topical authority.

## Decision-Block Style

Prefer questions like:

- "What changes the final flooring price?"
- "Why does underlay or acoustic layer matter?"
- "What should I check before accepting a flooring quote?"
- "When is a floor plan useful?"
- "Why can two flooring quotes look different?"

Avoid weak questions like:

- "Do you do flooring?"
- "Are you good?"
- "Can I get a quote?"

## Protected Systems

Copy work must not change:

- quote form logic
- quote calculation logic
- pricing files
- product data
- floorplan logic
- backend/API/email logic
- product interaction sections

If copy requires a logic change, stop and ask for explicit approval.
