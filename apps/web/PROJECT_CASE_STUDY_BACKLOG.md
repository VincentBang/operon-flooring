## Purpose

Real project pages should strengthen local SEO, trust, product confidence, and quote conversion. They must be based on genuine work or verified project details.

Each project candidate needs:

- suburb
- property type

- timeline
- visual outcome
- before/after photos if available
- internal links to product, suburb, quote, and guide pages

## Scope Standard Case Study Fields

Every future case study should collect enough detail to show real scope clarity without inventing claims.

Capture:

- product definition: category, brand/range/colour, thickness and plank size if known
- area and measurement: measured area, room count and stair details if applicable
- installation scope: supply/install split, method, underlay/acoustic layer and furniture handling
- site and access: house/apartment, level, lift, parking/loading and strata restrictions if relevant
- risk and preparation: existing floor, removal/disposal, subfloor condition, levelling and moisture notes
- finishing/accessories: trims, scotia, skirting, stair nosing, door trimming and transitions
- commercial clarity: whether GST, exclusions, validity and variation conditions were clarified
- final confirmation: what was checked before the work was booked

Use these fields to show operational clarity, not to create long case-study pages.

## Task W-C-01: Real Project Proof Intake Checklist

Use this checklist before a project candidate moves from backlog to draft. If any required item is missing, keep the project unpublished and record the missing evidence instead of filling gaps with assumptions.

Required intake:

- project identifier: internal job name, date range, and who confirmed the details
- suburb and property type: house, unit, apartment, townhouse, terrace, retail or other
- flooring category: laminate, hybrid, engineered timber, repair/replacement or mixed scope
- product evidence: brand, range, colour, thickness, plank size and warranty notes where known
- measured scope: measured area, room count, stairs, wastage assumption and any excluded areas
- existing floor: material removed, condition, disposal scope and whether furniture handling was included
- preparation notes: subfloor condition, levelling, moisture, acoustic, expansion or access issues
- finishing details: trims, scotia, skirting, transitions, stair nosing and door trimming
- access details: parking, loading, lift, stairs, building rules, strata or work-hour constraints
- decision context: why the customer chose the product or scope, such as durability, budget, finish, pets, rental update, apartment compliance or faster turnaround
- outcome evidence: approved before/after photos, close-up finish photos, permission status and any private details to exclude
- conversion links: intended product page, suburb page, guide page, quote page and quote-review path

Minimum publishable standard:

- enough verified detail to explain the real scope
- at least one usable project photo or visual proof with permission
- no invented testimonial, rating, suburb claim, product claim or outcome claim
- one practical buyer lesson tied to scope clarity, preparation, access, product choice or quote transparency

## Task W-C-02: Photo, Permission and Privacy Rules

Project proof should build trust without exposing customer details or exaggerating the work.

Photo rules:

- prefer owner-supplied or installer-supplied photos taken during the real project
- label photo status as `available`, `needs permission`, `not available`, or `do not use`
- capture the room type, stage and angle for each photo, such as before, during prep, finished floor, stair detail, transition detail or product close-up
- do not use supplier swatches, stock imagery or generic room photos as proof of completed work
- do not crop out problems in a way that misrepresents the scope, preparation or outcome

Permission and privacy rules:

- confirm permission before publishing identifiable homes, unit numbers, faces, license plates, documents or personal belongings
- remove customer names, addresses, quote totals and private building details unless explicitly approved
- avoid naming strata buildings unless permission is explicit and useful
- if proof is real but permission is incomplete, keep it as an internal candidate only
